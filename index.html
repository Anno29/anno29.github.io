🧰 Main Features

🔧 Car Setups

Create a setup sheet for each car and track combination. Record tyre pressures, camber, toe, spring rates, damper settings, ride height, brake bias, differential and gearing in clearly labelled fields. Write a short description of how the car felt so you remember the reason behind each change, not just the numbers.

🏠 Garage Profiles

Every car in your garage gets its own profile page. Keep the car name, class, favourite tracks, preferred setup and your own notes together. When you switch cars, you switch profiles and everything you learned about that machine is right there.

🗺️ Track Notes

Write notes corner by corner in your own words. Mark your braking points, the gears you use, kerbs to avoid and the places where you keep losing time. Notes are searchable, so the answer to "what was that tip for the second sector?" is a couple of keystrokes away.

⏱️ Lap Tracking

Log your lap times after each session and keep the history attached to the right car and track. The toolkit shows your best lap, your average and your most recent runs side by side, so improvement is easy to see instead of easy to imagine.

🎯 Driving Practice

Build simple practice plans. Choose a track, choose one skill to work on, set a target lap and give yourself a session length. A plan turns a vague evening of driving into something with a beginning and an end.

📊 Racing Analytics

The analytics view turns your saved numbers into readable summaries: consistency per track, which setups produced your fastest laps, and where your pace has stalled. No spreadsheets, no formulas, just clear results.

🕹️ Your First Session, Step by Step

  1. Open the toolkit and create your first garage profile with the car you drive most often.
  2. Add the track you know best and write three quick notes about it — even short ones are useful.
  3. Enter one setup sheet with the current settings, so you have a baseline to compare against later.
  4. Do a short practice run in your racing game as normal, then log the lap times you set.
  5. Change one setup value, run again, and log the new laps. Repeat once more if you have time.
  6. Open the analytics view and compare. You now have a real answer instead of a feeling.

💡 Practical Tips

  • Change one thing at a time. Two changes at once and you cannot tell which one helped.
  • Write notes while they are fresh. A sentence right after a session is worth a page written a week later.
  • Keep a baseline setup untouched. It is your reference point when you get lost.
  • Log every lap, including the slow ones. Slow laps explain fast laps.
  • Back up your toolkit data folder now and then. A copy on a USB stick takes seconds and saves hours.
